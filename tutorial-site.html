Deep Graph Models for Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

A 3-hour tutorial at ICVGIP 2026 · [Date] · [Venue]

Abstract

In this tutorial we will show how graph based models can be integrated with deep learning algorithms to solve a wide variety of problems in Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR). The integration itself can be imparted in different ways. For example, a minimum cut or a random walk on a graph can follow a deep model like UNet or GoogleNet. Alternatively, loss function appearing in a deep network can be improved through regularizers, drawing inspiration from the structure of the energy function in a graph cut. It is also a popular practice to apply deep learning on graph structured data in the form of a Graph Convolution Network (GCN), which falls under the broad umbrella of Graph Neural Network (GNN). Example problems have been carefully selected from two different domains, namely, multimedia (e.g. action recognition and action anticipation in unimodal and multimodal egocentric video) and biomedical image analysis (e.g. segmentation of brain tumor in MRI and brain cells in microscopic data) to demonstrate the application of these different deep graph models. With comprehensive experiments performance benefits from using deep graph models over state-of-the-art competitors, as well as their advantages over deep models or graph models used in isolation, will be evident. While the lectures would provide rich theoretical foundations and rationale, the demos would impart valuable knowhow.

Goals

  1. To show a synergistic combination of deep learning and graph-based methods as an innovative computational model in CVPR.
  2. To discuss various forms of integration of deep and graph models, with strong theoretical foundations and clear rationale behind the integration.
  3. To present multiple applications in two different domains, multimedia and biomedical image analysis, to illustrate the utility of deep graph models.

Learning outcomes

  1. Exposure to a new computational paradigm in CVPR.
  2. Theoretical understanding of various deep graph models.
  3. Knowing how to apply different models for practical problem solving.

Organizers

Prof. Ananda S. Chowdhury

Electronics & Telecommunication Engineering, Jadavpur University, Kolkata, India

Presenter

Ananda S. Chowdhury is a Professor and former Head in the department of Electronics and Telecommunication Engineering at Jadavpur University, Kolkata, India, where he leads the Imaging, Vision and Pattern Recognition group. He earned his Ph.D. in Computer Science from The University of Georgia, USA in July 2007. He then worked as a post-doctoral fellow in the department of Radiology and Imaging Sciences at National Institutes of Health, USA during the period August 2007 to December 2008. His research interests span Computer Vision, Pattern Recognition, Biomedical Image/Signal Processing, and Multimedia Analysis. Dr. Chowdhury has published more than one hundred papers in leading international journals and conferences in addition to two monographs, from Morgan Kaufmann, Elsevier, and ACVR, Springer. He is an IAPR TC-member of Graph based Representations in Pattern Recognition, a life member of IUPRAI, and a Fellow of the West Bengal Academy of Science and Technology. He currently serves IEEE Transactions on Image Processing as a Senior Area Editor, and Pattern Recognition Letters as an Area Editor. He is a Distinguished Visitor of the IEEE Computer Society. His Erdős Number is 2.

Computer Science & Engineering, Motilal Nehru National Institute of Technology (MNNIT), Allahabad, India
